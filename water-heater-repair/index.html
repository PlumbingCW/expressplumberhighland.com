Emergency Water Heater Repair in Highland, CA

Cold showers, a pooling tank or a pilot that will not stay lit: we diagnose first and tell you if repair beats replacement.

Call (909) 505-3225All Services

No hot water is an emergency in Highland, CA, and a leaking tank is worse. Repairing a gas or electric tank water heater should start with a diagnosis so you pay to fix the real fault and not a guess.

While you wait, shut the cold water supply valve on top of the heater if you see a leak, and turn the gas control to off or flip the breaker for an electric unit. Keep children and pets away from standing water near the tank.

Start With the Symptom, Not the Part

Callers rarely say the heating element failed. They say the shower runs cold after two minutes, the tank rumbles, or there is water on the garage floor. Each symptom points to a short list of causes, and the plumber tests them in order.

A repair is worth doing when the tank is sound and the failed part is cheap. It stops being worth it when the tank itself is leaking, because a tank cannot be patched. Ask the plumber to tell you which case you are in before any parts are ordered.

Signs a Repair Will Not Hold

Some symptoms mean the tank is near the end of its life and money spent on parts is wasted.

  • Rust-colored hot waterCorrosion inside the tank, not just old pipes.
  • Water at the tank base that is not from a fittingThe tank wall has failed.
  • Tank age past a decadeTank heaters commonly run roughly 8 to 12 years.
  • Repeated repairs in one yearEach fix is a sign the next failure is close.
  • Anode rod gone and no flush historyThe tank lining has lost its protection.
  • Pooling in a pan or on a subfloorWater is already damaging what sits below.

Hard Water and Tank Wear in Highland

East Valley Water District's 2026 report lists an average hardness of 152 mg/L, which is about 8.9 grains per gallon by the standard conversion. Hard water leaves mineral scale on heating elements and the tank floor, so electric elements burn out sooner and gas tanks run louder and less efficiently.

That is why an annual flush matters more here than in a soft-water town. If your tank has never been flushed, ask the plumber to check the anode rod and drain a little sediment at the same visit, which tells you how much life the tank has left.

Gas water heaters are installed according to the manufacturer's instructions and the plumbing code, which is based on the UPC, so a repair should leave the venting, the relief valve and its drain as the code intends.

Gas Units, Venting and Safety Checks

A gas heater that will not stay lit is a safety question as well as a comfort one. We test the thermocouple, the gas control and the draft at the vent before relighting. If you smell gas, leave the house and call your gas utility or 911 first, then call us.

The City of Highland's water heater installation bulletin sets rules for vent offsets, combustion air openings and the height of burners in a garage, and any repair that touches those items has to respect them. Ask whether the installer knows the bulletin and checks the installation.

What to do before help arrives

If the heater leaks, smells of gas or makes noise, these steps protect you.

  1. Smell for gas

    If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility from outside.

  2. Turn off the heater

    Set the gas control to off or switch off the breaker.

  3. Close the cold water supply

    The shutoff is on the pipe above the tank.

  4. Do not touch hot discharge

    The relief pipe can release scalding water.

  5. Clear the area

    Move items away from the heater and the drain pan.
