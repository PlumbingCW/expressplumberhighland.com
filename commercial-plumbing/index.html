Commercial plumbing in Highland, CA covers the pipes, drains, water heaters and fixtures that keep a business open, from a restaurant grease line to an office restroom. When a line fails during business hours or overnight, call right away to stop the damage and get you operating again.

If water is running where it should not, close the nearest shutoff or the main valve, move stock and equipment away from the wet area, and call. Tell the person who answers the type of business, because a kitchen needs different equipment from an office.

Why Business Plumbing Is Different

A home has a few bathrooms and a kitchen. A business can have a dozen fixtures on one line, a grease interceptor, a large water heater and a landlord, a tenant and an insurer who all need to know what happened.

That is why we work from a written record. After each call we note what failed, what we did and what remains, so the owner, the tenant and the insurer all read the same story.

Highland Businesses and Local Rules

Highland has commercial corridors along Base Line and Boulder Avenue, close to Arden Avenue, and many smaller shops and offices sit in older buildings with original plumbing. The City of Highland's Building and Safety Division is the permit authority, and its permit exceptions page lists what work does and does not need a permit. Ask before replacing concealed pipe.

Water service comes from East Valley Water District, which also publishes its own rules. If a business has a backflow device, the district may require periodic testing, so ask your plumber to help you stay current.

Commercial kitchens and restrooms must meet the plumbing code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and grease interceptors are covered by that code as well as by local wastewater rules.

Restaurants and Grease

Restaurants generate grease that cools and sticks in pipes. A grease trap or interceptor, where one is installed, must be emptied on a regular schedule or the line downstream will block. Hydro jetting clears the line between cleanings. See hydro jetting for the process.

Floor drains are another weak point. They collect food particles and dry out, which can let sewer gas back into the kitchen. We can clean them and show you what we found.

Landlords, Tenants and Property Managers

When a tenant calls about a leak, the landlord often has to approve the work. We can send findings and a price to the owner directly, and we note which part of the system the repair affects.

If a property needs recurring work, such as a quarterly drain clean, we can suggest a schedule after the first visit. You can read about related work on our sewer line cleaning and water heater replacement pages.

What to do before the plumber arrives

Whoever is on site can limit the damage in a few minutes.

  1. Close the nearest shutoff

    Or the main valve, if water is flowing.

  2. Stop using affected fixtures

    Post a sign on restrooms and kitchen sinks.

  3. Move stock and equipment

    Lift items off wet floors and away from ceilings.

  4. Keep people clear

    Wet floors are a slip hazard, and sewage is a health hazard.

  5. Photograph the area

    Take photos for the landlord and the insurer before cleanup.
