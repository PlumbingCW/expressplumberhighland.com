Water Softener and Filtration in Highland, CA

Installation, repair and sizing for softeners and filters, matched to Highland's water.

Water softener and filtration service in Highland, CA starts with your water. East Valley Water District's 2026 report lists average hardness at 152 mg/L, which is about 8.9 grains per gallon by conversion. That is hard enough to leave scale on fixtures and shorten the life of a water heater.

Plumbers install, repair and size softeners and filters. Ask about timing when you call, and ask for the price before work starts.

What Hard Water Does in a Home

Calcium and magnesium leave white scale on faucets and glass, spot dishes and reduce soap lather. Inside, scale narrows pipes and coats the heating element or the bottom of a tank water heater, which makes it work harder.

At 152 mg/L, the effect is gradual. Many homeowners first notice spotting and a weak shower head. A water heater that is flushed rarely may hold a thick layer of sediment by the time it fails.

Signs You Might Want a Softener

These signs are easy to see.

  • White crust on faucets and showerheadsMineral scale.
  • Spotted glasses and dishesMinerals left after drying.
  • Dry skin or stiff laundrySoap does not rinse cleanly.
  • Falling shower pressureScale in aerators and pipes.
  • A water heater that makes popping soundsSediment under the heating element.
  • Frequent fixture replacementScale wears seals and cartridges.

Highland's Water Source

East Valley Water District reports that about 78 percent of its supply in 2026 came from Bunker Hill Basin groundwater, with the remaining 22 percent from the State Water Project and Santa Ana River water treated at Plant 134. Groundwater picks up minerals as it moves through the basin, which explains the hardness.

The district's report is the best place to see current water quality data. You can compare it with your own test strips to size a system. Local softener discharge rules may apply, so ask us about drain connections.

Softener drain connections need an air gap according to the plumbing code, which is based on the UPC, so that wastewater cannot siphon back into the unit.

Maintenance and Salt

A salt-based softener needs salt added every few weeks, and the brine tank should be cleaned yearly. Most units regenerate on a schedule or by demand.

If a softener stops making soft water, the cause is often empty salt, a clogged injector or a stuck valve. Ask whether the plumber services your brand. See also water heater repair if scale has affected your heater.

Sizing a System to Your Household

Softener size follows the number of people, the hardness reading and how much water you use. A unit that is too small regenerates too often and wastes salt. One that is too large may sit idle and develop odor.

We test the water at your tap, ask how many people live in the home and look at where the unit can go. Then we recommend a capacity and a style, and we explain the maintenance.
