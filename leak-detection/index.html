Emergency Leak Detection in Highland, CA

We find hidden leaks behind walls, under floors and in buried lines using thermal imaging, acoustic tools and meter tests.

Call (909) 505-3225All Services

Leak detection in Highland, CA means locating the exact source of a hidden water leak with electronic tools, so the repair opens one small spot and not half your house. If your water meter keeps turning with every tap off, or a wall is wet and warm, treat it as an emergency, so call right away.

While you wait for a plumber, shut off the main water valve at the house and see whether the meter stops. If it does, the leak is inside your system. If it keeps moving, note the reading and tell the person who answers, because that points to the line between the meter and the house.

Start With the Symptom You Can See

Most calls begin with a symptom and not with the words leak detection. These are the ones we hear most.

  • The meter moves with everything offWater is leaving your system somewhere.
  • A water bill that jumps with no change in habitsA slow leak or a running toilet can add up quickly.
  • A warm spot on the floorA hot water line may be leaking under the slab.
  • A stain or bubble on a ceiling or wallA pipe above or inside the wall is wet.
  • The sound of running water at nightWater is flowing when nothing is on.
  • Low water pressureA leak upstream steals pressure from the fixtures.

Where Hidden Leaks Hide

Behind walls and ceilings

Supply lines and drain traps leak inside wall cavities and show up as stains, odor or soft drywall. Thermal imaging shows temperature differences, and a moisture meter confirms the wet area before anyone cuts drywall.

Under the house or slab

A leak in a crawlspace or under a slab can run for months without any sign upstairs. Acoustic listening and pressure testing narrow it down to a few feet. Our slab leak repair page covers what happens after we find it.

In buried lines and irrigation

A service line between the meter and the house, or an irrigation line in the yard, can leak with no surface sign. Soggy lawn patches, a green stripe over the pipe or a meter that keeps running are the clues. A leaking sprinkler line is easier to fix than a main service line, so finding it first matters.

What a High Bill Looks Like in Highland

East Valley Water District serves the City of Highland, and a leak at your property shows up as a rise in your bill. You can check your own meter by turning off every fixture and watching the leak indicator or low-flow dial. If it moves, water is going somewhere.

Galvanized steel supply lines can corrode from inside and leak at the threads, while copper tends to leak at fittings or through a pinhole. Knowing which pipe you have tells you where to listen first, and a pressure test shows which case applies to your home.

Pressure testing follows the same idea as the test standards in the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC): isolate a section of pipe and see whether it holds.

Water Meter and High-Bill Leaks

A high water bill is the second most common reason people call. We start by isolating the system at the main valve and watching the meter. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house or yard system. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the shutoff.

From there we check toilets for silent leaks, test irrigation, and listen along the supply line. Many high-bill leaks turn out to be a toilet flapper or a sprinkler valve and cost little to fix. We tell you what we find before you spend anything on repair.

Pool, Spa and Commercial Properties

Pool and spa leaks need different equipment from house leaks, because a water level dropping can be evaporation. We check the plumbing lines and the equipment pad when the leak is in the supply or return side.

Property managers and tenants can call for commercial leak detection too. We provide findings in writing so the owner and the insurer have what they need. See our commercial plumbing page for ongoing service.
