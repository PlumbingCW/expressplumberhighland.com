Remodels and Additions

A remodel is easier on plumbing when the plumber is involved before the walls close. Moving a sink or adding a bathroom means new supply and drain lines, and drains need slope and venting to work.

We review the plan with you, explain what needs a permit and rough in the work so your inspector can sign off before the finish work begins.

Permits and Standards in Highland

The City of Highland's permit exceptions page lists minor work that does not require a permit, including repairing or replacing a faucet or fixture without altering the drain or supply lines. New lines, relocated fixtures and added bathrooms are new work and generally need a permit and inspection through Building and Safety.

Your plumber should tell you which category your job falls into. A job without a required permit can create problems when you sell, so ask early and keep the paperwork.

Installations follow the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the UPC, and that code sets fixture clearances, vent sizes and drain slopes.

Water Quality and Fixture Choice

East Valley Water District reports average hardness of 152 mg/L in 2026, which leaves scale on fixtures. Finishes that hide spots and fixtures with removable aerators are easier to keep clean in hard water.

If scale is a concern throughout the home, see water softener and filtration before you choose fixtures.

Appliance Hookups

Washing machines need two supply valves, a drain standpipe with a trap and a slope that carries water away. Dishwashers need a supply, a drain connection with a high loop or air gap and an electrical connection. Ice makers need a supply line with a shutoff.

We check that shutoffs work, that hoses are rated for the use and that the drain can keep up. A leak from a washing machine hose is a common source of water damage, so we replace worn hoses during the visit.

Planning Rough In Work

A bathroom remodel has two plumbing stages. The rough in sets drains, vents and supply lines before walls close. The finish stage sets fixtures after tile and paint. Gaps between the two stages are common, and a plumber who comes back at the right time saves delays.

Share your fixture list, the shower valve model and any wall niche or bench plans early. Fixtures set height and clearance, and a late change can mean opening a finished wall.
