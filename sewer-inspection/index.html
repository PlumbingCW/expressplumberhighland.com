Sewer Camera Inspection in Highland, CA

A camera runs through your sewer line so you see roots, cracks and bellies on video before you spend on repairs.

Call (909) 505-3225All Services

A sewer camera inspection in Highland, CA sends a waterproof camera through your drain line to show its real condition, in color, on a screen. It answers the questions a cable cannot: is the pipe cracked, are roots inside, does water pool in a low spot.

Inspections suit home buyers, owners with repeat clogs and anyone planning a remodel. Ask about timing when you call, and ask for the price before work starts.

What the Camera Shows

The camera head has its own light and records as it moves. We note the distance from the access point to each finding, so a repair can be located on the surface.

Typical findings include root intrusion at joints, cracks, offset joints, a belly where water pools, scale buildup and collapsed sections. We also confirm whether the line connects to the city main correctly.

When an Inspection Is Worth Booking

These situations are the best reasons to scope a line.

  • Buying an older homeSewer lines are out of sight and costly to repair.
  • Repeat clogsA clog that returns means a cause.
  • Before a remodelNew fixtures put more load on the line.
  • After a backupFind the reason before it happens again.
  • Slow drains throughoutA main line problem is likely.
  • Wet spots in the yardA broken pipe may be leaking.

Older Neighborhoods and Newer Lots in Highland

Highland is a city of about 57,000 people at the base of the San Bernardino Mountains, with citrus-era streets near Palm Avenue and newer subdivisions farther east. A scope tells you which pipe you own, and the age and material matter when you plan a repair or a purchase.

If you are buying an older building, ask for a scope of the lateral. Pipe material depends on when the building was plumbed, and a recording gives you a basis for negotiating or planning. The Highland Historic District page explains the City preservation rules that apply near Palm Avenue.

A camera does not replace the inspection a city performs for a permit. It gives you a record, and the plumbing code, which is based on the UPC, still governs any repair.

How an Inspection Visit Works

The visit is short and non-invasive.

  1. Find access

    We locate the cleanout or pull a fixture to reach the line.

  2. Run the camera

    The head travels to the connection, recording as it goes.

  3. Mark findings

    Problem spots are logged by distance.

  4. Review

    Ask for an explanation of the footage, the repair options and a price.

Reading the Footage Together

After the scope we play the video and point out what we see. We mark the distance of each defect from the cleanout, so you understand where the problem sits on your property. Ask for a copy of the footage if you plan to share it with a seller, a buyer or an insurer.

Not every blemish needs repair. A small root hair or a mineral line may only need cleaning. A crack, an offset or a belly deserves a plan.
