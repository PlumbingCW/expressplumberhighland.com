Water Heater Installation in Highland, CA

New construction, an addition or a planned upgrade: tank and tankless units set to Highland's installation bulletin.

Water heater installation in Highland, CA covers a new unit going where none existed, a move to a new location, or a planned upgrade before the old tank fails. The installer sets the heater, connects water, gas or power, and finishes the safety items the City of Highland expects.

If you are planning ahead, book a daytime slot and bring the questions on this page. Ask about timing when you call, and ask for the price before work starts.

Where the Heater Goes Matters

Location sets half the job. A garage unit needs burners raised off the floor and protection from vehicles. An attic or closet unit needs a drain pan. A hallway closet needs combustion air openings sized to the code.

We walk the space before quoting, because moving a heater even a few feet changes vent length, gas pipe and drain routing. This is also the time to decide whether a tankless unit is a better fit for your layout.

Installation Details the City Expects

The City of Highland's April 2017 water heater bulletin lists these items. Your plumber should address each one.

  • Seismic strapsWithin the upper and lower thirds of the tank, with at least 4 inches above the controls to the lower strap.
  • Drain pan1.5 inch deep, corrosion resistant, piped through at least a 3/4 inch drain in attics and on subfloors.
  • Garage clearanceBurners and ignition devices at least 18 inches above the floor unless listed otherwise.
  • Combustion airTwo permanent openings, each at least 100 square inches, within 12 inches of the top and bottom.
  • Relief valve drainTerminates outside between 6 and 24 inches above grade, no smaller than the valve outlet.
  • Type B ventMostly vertical with offsets of 45 degrees or less, and a termination at least 5 feet above the heater.

Highland Water and Your New Heater

East Valley Water District reports average hardness of 152 mg/L, and about 78 percent of its supply comes from groundwater from the Bunker Hill Basin. Hard groundwater leaves scale on heating surfaces, so we install a shutoff valve and a flush valve that make yearly maintenance easy.

If you plan a water softener, install it before the heater. Putting softened water into a new heater from day one protects the warranty period and the efficiency of the unit.

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) that California's plumbing code is based on sets standards for relief valves, drains and venting, and the City of Highland's bulletin applies them to local installations.

Permit and inspectionAsk the City whether your project needs one and what it costs.
Expansion tank and pressureClosed systems may need an expansion tank, and the City bulletin does not cover this, so we check with Building and Safety.
