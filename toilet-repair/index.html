Toilet repair in Highland, CA covers clogs, leaks at the base, tanks that will not stop running and bowls that rock on the floor. An overflowing toilet or a leak at the base is urgent, so call right away.

If water is rising, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. That stops new water and gives you time to call.

Stop an Overflow Right Now

These steps take a minute and limit the damage.

  1. Stop the water

    Turn the valve behind the toilet clockwise, or lift the float arm.

  2. Do not flush again

    A second flush adds water to a full bowl.

  3. Protect the floor

    Put towels around the base and open a window if you can.

  4. Call us

    Tell us if the water is clear or contaminated.

Common Toilet Problems and Causes

The symptom usually points to the cause.

  • Weak flushClogged rim jets, a low water level or a partial clog.
  • Running tankA worn flapper or fill valve.
  • Water around the baseA failed wax ring or loose closet bolts.
  • Rocking toiletLoose bolts or a damaged flange.
  • Phantom flushingA flapper that leaks slowly.
  • Frequent clogsA partial blockage in the trap or the line.

Leaks at the Base and Under the Floor

A toilet that leaks at the base is sitting on a failed seal. Water seeps into the subfloor, which can rot wood and stain the ceiling below. Do not keep using it.

We pull the toilet, inspect the flange and the subfloor, replace the wax ring and set the toilet back. If the subfloor is soft, we tell you before we reinstall.

Hard Water and Toilet Parts in Highland

East Valley Water District's 2026 report lists average hardness at 152 mg/L. Hard water leaves mineral buildup in the tank, the fill valve and the rim jets. A flush that has grown weak over months is often a scale problem, not a mechanical failure.

Cleaning the rim jets and replacing a scaled fill valve restores flow in many cases. For a home with a softener, see water softener and filtration.

California sets water efficiency standards for new toilets, so a replacement should be a high efficiency model, and the plumbing code, based on the UPC, governs how it is set and sealed.

When It Is Not the Toilet

If several fixtures drain slowly or gurgle, the clog is in the line. Plunging the toilet again only wastes effort. We can scope the line and clear it. See drain cleaning.

Wipes marketed as flushable cause many toilet and line clogs. The only things that belong in the toilet are waste and toilet paper.

Preventing Clogs and Leaks

Flush only waste and toilet paper. Replace a flapper that is more than a few years old before it fails, and replace braided supply lines that are corroded at the fittings. A quick look under the tank every few months catches small leaks before they harm the floor.

If a toilet needs frequent plunging, the cause may be a partial clog in the trap or the line. A plumber can clear it with a closet auger or a camera, and tell you whether the toilet itself is the weak link.
