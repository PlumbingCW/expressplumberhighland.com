Whole-House Repiping in Highland, CA

Replace failing supply lines with modern pipe, with a clear plan, a written price and minimal wall opening.

Call (909) 505-3225All Services

Whole-house repiping in Highland, CA replaces the supply lines that bring water to your fixtures, and it is the right answer when old pipes keep leaking or turn the water brown. It is a larger project than a repair, so we explain the options and give a written price before any wall is opened.

If a pipe is actively leaking now, shut off the main valve and call. A repiping plan can follow once the emergency is handled.

Signs It May Be Time to Repipe

One leak is a repair. These patterns suggest the system is wearing out.

  • Repeat pinhole leaksPipes are corroding through.
  • Brown or rust-tinted waterSteel pipe is corroding inside.
  • Falling water pressureMineral and rust narrow the pipe.
  • Visible green or white crust on copperCorrosion at joints.
  • A home with original galvanized steelThese lines are well past their expected life.
  • Known problem pipe typesSome plastic pipe from certain eras is prone to failure.

Pipe Options

PEX

Flexible plastic that bends around corners, which means fewer fittings and fewer wall openings. It resists scale and handles minor freeze expansion, and it is the most common choice for whole-house repipes.

Copper

Long-lasting and familiar, copper costs more and takes more labor. Aggressive water or certain soil conditions can cause pinhole leaks, so we review your water first.

Spot repair instead

If only one section is failing, replacing that run may be enough. We tell you when a full repipe is not needed.

Highland Housing and Water Chemistry

Highland grew from citrus groves into a suburb after the townsite was surveyed in 1891 and the city incorporated in 1987, so homes range widely in age. Age gives a hint about pipe type, but only an inspection confirms it.

East Valley Water District's 2026 report lists average hardness at 152 mg/L, and about 78 percent of its supply is groundwater from the Bunker Hill Basin. Hard water builds scale inside pipe over time, so older lines often have a narrowed inside diameter. A repipe restores that diameter.

Pipe used for drinking water must be listed for that use under the standards the plumbing code references, so ask which listing your new pipe carries and keep the paperwork.

How a Repipe Goes

A repipe is planned to keep your home usable.

  1. Assess

    We inspect the system, test pressure and review pipe type.

  2. Plan and price

    You get a written scope, a price and a schedule.

  3. Permit

    We arrange the permit and inspection where required.

  4. Replace

    We run new lines, reconnect fixtures and patch access openings.

  5. Test and flush

    We pressure test, flush the system and check every fixture.

Permits, Inspection and Drywall

The City of Highland's permit exceptions page lists the repairs that do not require a permit. A full repipe is new work and typically requires a permit and inspection through Building and Safety.

Repiping needs access to walls. We plan the openings to be small and put access in closets and garages where we can. Drywall patching is part of the plan, and we tell you what is included before work starts.

What to do if a pipe fails before the repipe

Aging pipe can fail at any hour, so know the steps.

  • Shut off the main valveDo it at once if water is spraying.
  • Open a low faucetRelieves pressure and drains the line.
  • Keep clear of wet outletsDo not touch electrical items while standing in water.
  • Photograph the damageYour insurer will ask.
  • Call for a plumberTell the person who answers the pipe type if you know it.
