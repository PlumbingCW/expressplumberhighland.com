Why Tankless Units Need Flushing in Hard Water

A tankless heater heats water as it flows through a narrow heat exchanger. Dissolved minerals in hard water bake onto that surface and shrink the passage. The unit then works harder, throws error codes and delivers less hot water.

A descaling flush circulates a mild acid solution through the exchanger to dissolve the scale. Most manufacturers recommend a regular schedule, and in hard water that schedule needs to be tighter. Flush calls are the most common reason homeowners phone about tankless units.

Flush and Descale: What the Visit Includes

We isolate the unit with its service valves, connect a pump and bucket, and circulate descaling solution through the exchanger. We then rinse, clear the inlet screen and check the flame and flow readings.

The service takes a short visit, and we recommend a schedule based on your water. In Highland that usually means more often than the manufacturer's baseline. If your home has no isolation valves, we add them so the next flush is simpler.

A Hard Water Reading That Changes the Math

The East Valley Water District report puts Highland's average hardness at 152 mg/L, roughly 8.9 grains per gallon by the standard conversion. Water at that level is firmly in the hard range, so scale management should be part of any tankless plan from day one.

A water softener ahead of the heater reduces scale at the source. A homeowner who does not want a softener can follow a descaling schedule instead. We help you pick based on how long you plan to stay in the home.

Tankless units follow the manufacturer's venting and gas sizing standards, and their electrical connections follow the National Electrical Code (NEC) as adopted in California.

Installing a Tankless Heater

A tankless install touches gas, venting, water and often electrical, so the sequence matters.

  1. Check capacity

    We confirm gas line size, vent route and electrical supply.

  2. Plan the connections

    Isolation valves, a relief valve and a condensate path are set before the unit goes up.

  3. Mount and connect

    The unit is mounted, vented and connected to water and gas or power.

  4. Commission

    We test flow, temperature and combustion and walk you through the controls.
