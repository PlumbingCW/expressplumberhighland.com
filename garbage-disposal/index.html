Garbage Disposal Repair and Installation in Highland, CA

Jams, leaks, clogs and new installations. Ask for the price before work begins.

Call (909) 505-3225All Services

Garbage disposal repair in Highland, CA covers jams, humming motors, leaks and slow-draining kitchen sinks. Many disposal problems are simple, and a few are signs it should be replaced.

Before you call, turn the disposal off at the wall switch and never put your hand in the unit. Ask about timing when you call.

Common Disposal Problems

The sound or symptom tells us where to look.

  • Humming but not spinningThe blades are jammed.
  • Nothing happensA tripped reset or an electrical issue.
  • Leak under the sinkA failed seal, a loose flange or a cracked housing.
  • Slow draining sinkA clog downstream of the unit.
  • Bad smellFood buildup in the chamber.
  • Loud grindingForeign objects or worn parts.

Safe First Steps

These steps cost nothing and can fix a jam.

  1. Turn it off

    Use the wall switch, and unplug it if you can.

  2. Look for the hex socket

    Many units have a hole at the bottom for an Allen wrench to free the jam.

  3. Press the reset

    A red button on the bottom resets a tripped motor.

  4. Call if it persists

    Leaks and repeated jams need a plumber.

Repair or Replace

A jam or a reset is a quick fix. A leak from the housing, a seized motor or a unit more than a decade old is usually worth replacing. A new disposal costs little compared with repeated repairs.

Ask whether the replacement can be installed on the same visit. Tell us whether you want a basic unit or one with more power for heavy use.

Highland Kitchens and Drain Lines

A disposal pushes more solid waste into the kitchen drain, and rough or older interior walls hold onto grease.

If your sink drains slowly after the disposal is cleared, the line behind it may need cleaning. Our drain cleaning page explains the options. East Valley Water District's hard water also leaves scale on the disposal's drain parts.

Disposal wiring follows the National Electrical Code (NEC) as adopted in California, and the drain connection follows the trap rules in the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Installation Details

Installing a disposal means mounting the flange, connecting the drain, wiring or plugging in the unit and, where a dishwasher is present, opening the dishwasher knockout so water can drain. A missed knockout is a common cause of a dishwasher that will not empty.

We test the unit, run water through it and check below the sink for leaks before we leave.

Noise, Odor and Slow Draining

A grinding sound often means a foreign object is in the chamber. A humming sound with no motion means a jam. A clanking sound can be a loose mounting ring or a spoon in the unit.

Odors come from food film on the splash guard and the chamber walls. Cleaning with ice, citrus and a brush often helps. If the odor persists, the drain line behind the disposal may be coated and need cleaning.

Warranty and Lifespan

Many disposals last around a decade, and some less with heavy use or hard water. Manufacturers often offer a warranty that covers the motor for a number of years. Keep the receipt and the model number.

If your unit is out of warranty and the repair price approaches the price of a new one, replacement is usually the better choice.
