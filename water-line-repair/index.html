Emergency Water Line Repair in Highland, CA

We repair or replace the line between the meter and your house, with trenching or trenchless options.

Call (909) 505-3225All Services

Water line repair in Highland, CA fixes the underground service line that carries water from the meter to your house. A break shows up as a soggy patch in the yard, a sudden drop in pressure or a meter that keeps running. Call to ask about timing.

Shut off the main valve at your house. If water still runs at the meter, the break is between the meter and the house. Call us, and also call 811 before any digging so utilities are marked.

Signs of a Failing Water Line

Most signs show up in the yard or at the tap.

  • A wet, soft or sunken spot in the yardWater is surfacing from the line.
  • Low pressure at every fixtureThe line is leaking before the house.
  • The meter moves with everything offWater is leaving the system.
  • A sudden high water billA leak is running constantly.
  • Dirty or discolored waterThe line is corroded or damaged.
  • Sound of rushing water near the meterA break in the line.

Where the Line Fails

Service lines fail at joints, at the meter connection and wherever roots, soil movement or old age have weakened the pipe. Galvanized steel corrodes, and older copper can develop pinholes.

Finding the exact spot saves digging. We use a pressure test and acoustic locator to narrow it, then open the smallest area that lets us reach the pipe.

Utilities and Rules in Highland

East Valley Water District owns the water system up to the meter, and the line from the meter to your house is generally the owner's responsibility. Call the district if you are not sure where the meter connection stops and your line starts.

Highland's Building and Safety Division can tell you whether your repair needs a permit. Call 811 before any digging so gas, electric and water lines are marked.

Service lines carry water under pressure, so the plumbing code, which is based on the UPC, calls for approved materials and a pressure test before the trench is closed.

What the Repair Looks Like

Digging is planned and limited.

  1. Shut off and test

    We isolate the line and confirm the break.

  2. Mark utilities

    811 marks buried lines before digging.

  3. Repair or replace

    We expose the pipe and make the repair.

  4. Test and backfill

    We pressure test, restore the soil and clean up.

Pressure, Valves and the Meter Connection

Not every pressure problem is a broken pipe. A partly closed main valve, a failing pressure regulator or a clogged filter can cut flow in the house. We check these first, since they cost less than digging.

If the meter connection is the problem, the district may need to be involved. We help you describe what you see so the right party is called, and we protect your side of the line while the work is done.
