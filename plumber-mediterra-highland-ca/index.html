The Plan Behind Mediterra

The Mediterra Planned Development Document, on the City of Highland website, describes a community of about 178.73 acres along Greenspot Road. It allows 316 homes across five planning areas and lists a document date of January 12, 2021.

New construction listings for homes in Mediterra appear on major real estate sites, so at least part of the plan has been built. The plan sets the maximum, and how many homes exist today is a question for the City.

The plan divides the community into five planning areas with different housing types.

What a Newer Home Changes

A newer home usually comes with paperwork. Builders and manufacturers often provide warranties on heaters, fixtures and the plumbing system, and the terms may depend on who does the work. Read the warranty before hiring anyone to alter a covered item.

Hard water still applies. East Valley Water District's 2026 report lists average hardness at 152 mg/L, which is about 8.9 grains per gallon. A new tank heater and new fixtures will collect scale just like old ones, and a softener or filter protects them from day one.

Where Mediterra Is

The planned development document places Mediterra along Greenspot Road, north and west of the Santa Ana River and south of the San Bernardino National Forest. The main entries are Mediterra Parkway and Terraneo Parkway, both off Greenspot Road. Residential neighborhoods of the East Highlands area lie to its west.

A Homeowner Checklist for the First Few Years

Small checks catch problems while a warranty may still apply.

  • Test the main water pressureA gauge on a hose bib shows the pressure, and the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, calls for a regulator where static pressure could exceed 80 psi.
  • Look at the heater's relief valve and drainThe discharge pipe should run downward and end at a safe spot.
  • Watch the meterIf it moves with every fixture off, there is a leak somewhere.
  • Check hose bibs and irrigationLots of 7,000 to 9,500 square feet mean more outdoor water use.
  • Flush the tank yearlyHard groundwater leaves scale in new tanks too.

Services Owners of Newer Homes Ask About

Leaks

A rising water bill in a new home is worth a leak test, because irrigation and hose bibs are common sources.

Fixtures

Dripping faucets and running toilets are common early calls. See faucet repair and toilet repair.

Additions

A home office, a bathroom or a utility sink needs plumbing installation work and usually a permit.

Permits and Utilities

The City of Highland Building and Safety Division is the permit authority, and its permit exceptions page lists work that needs no permit. Call 811 before any digging so buried lines are marked.

Check your bill to confirm which water provider serves your address. If your street has a homeowners association, read its rules before adding anything visible from the street.
