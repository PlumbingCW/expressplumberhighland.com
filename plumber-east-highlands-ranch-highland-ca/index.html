East Highlands Ranch is a planned unit development on the eastern side of Highland, California. The City of Highland Land Use Element lists it at 1,655 acres and says development there was spurred in the 1980s. That timeline matters for plumbing, because homes built over several decades have heaters, fixtures and drain lines of different ages.

This page explains what the public record says about the area and what general plumbing guidance fits a home like yours. To ask about a leak, a clog or a heater that has stopped working, call (909) 505-3225.

What the City Says About the Community

The City of Highland Land Use Element names East Highlands Ranch as a planned unit development, usually shortened to PUD. A PUD follows its own standards and guidelines, and the City's Community Design Element points to those standards when it describes keeping the character of the newer eastern subdivisions.

The same element says development in the area was spurred in the 1980s. A supplement to the East Highlands Ranch environmental report was received by the state on January 29, 1985, which fits that timeline. Later amendments to the PUD changed how some planning areas are used, so the community kept evolving after its first homes were built.

Water Heaters in a Community Built Over Decades

Because the PUD grew over many years, you cannot judge a heater by the neighborhood. Read the data plate on the tank. The serial number usually encodes the month and year of manufacture, and a tank heater commonly lasts roughly 8 to 12 years. A tank past that range, or one that rumbles or leaks at the base, deserves a closer look.

The City of Highland publishes its April 2017 Water Heater Installation Bulletin. It lists seismic strapping, a drain pan where a leak could damage finished space, an 18 inch ignition elevation in garages, a temperature and pressure relief drain, combustion air and venting. The bulletin does not address permits, so ask City Building and Safety at 27215 Baseline whether your replacement needs one.

Hard Water Reaches Every Home in the City

East Valley Water District publishes an annual Consumer Confidence Report, and the 2026 edition lists average hardness at 152 mg/L. By a standard conversion of 17.1 mg/L per grain, that is about 8.9 grains per gallon. Check your bill to confirm which provider serves your address.

Scale builds up inside heaters and on faucet screens, shower heads and valves. If a fixture crusts over or a heater is noisy, hardness is a likely contributor. A water softener or filter changes that, and a yearly flush of the tank slows the buildup.

Where East Highlands Ranch Sits

The City describes East Highlands Ranch as part of the eastern side of Highland, east of State Route 30. A City environmental record from January 1985 lists the project location at Church Street, south of the National Forest Service boundary. The map shows the general area, and the exact boundary of the planned unit development is set by the City's planning documents.

Safety Steps if a Water Heater Fails

A failing tank can scald, leak or leak gas, so act in this order.

  • Smell gasLeave the house and call the gas utility or 911 from outside before anything else.
  • See water poolingShut off the cold water supply to the tank and turn off the gas or power to the heater.
  • Hear the relief valve dischargingDo not cap the discharge pipe, and keep people clear of the hot water.
  • Find rust or dripping at the baseThe tank has likely failed, so plan for replacement and document the damage for your insurer.

Services Homeowners Often Ask About

Water heaters

When a tank leaks or will not heat, the choice is repair or replacement. The pages on water heater repair and water heater replacement explain how to decide.

Drains and lines

Repeat clogs usually point to something beyond the fixture. Drain cleaning clears the blockage and a camera shows what caused it.

Hidden leaks

A high water bill with no visible leak calls for leak detection, which finds the spot before anything is opened.

Permits, Utilities and Neighborhood Rules

The City of Highland Building and Safety Division is the permit authority, and its permit exceptions page lists work that does not need a permit. When in doubt, ask before the work starts. Call 811 before any digging so buried lines are marked.

If your street has a homeowners association, check its rules before changing anything visible from the street, such as a vent terminal or an equipment enclosure. The PUD guidelines and any association rules are separate from the City's plumbing rules.
