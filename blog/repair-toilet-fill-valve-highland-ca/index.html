How to Repair a Toilet Fill Valve in Highland, CA

By the Highland Express Plumbing team.Published November 13, 2026.Updated November 13, 2026.

To repair a toilet fill valve, shut off the water at the wall, flush to empty the tank, disconnect the supply line, unscrew the old valve from the bottom of the tank and install a new one at the same height. Most homeowners finish in under an hour with an adjustable wrench and a new valve.

If you cannot stop the water or the tank is cracked, close the shutoff valve and call a plumber. Timing depends on the plumber and the job, so call and ask. A same-day visit is not promised.

How to Tell the Fill Valve Is the Problem

A fill valve controls how the tank refills after each flush. These symptoms usually point to it.

  • Hissing after the flush endsThe valve is not closing fully.
  • Water trickling into the overflow tubeThe water level is set too high.
  • The tank refills slowlyThe valve is clogged with debris or scale.
  • The toilet runs every few minutesWater is escaping into the bowl.
  • A loud banging as the tank fillsThe valve is worn or the supply is too forceful.

Replace the Fill Valve Step by Step

You need a new fill valve, a bucket, a sponge and an adjustable wrench. A universal valve fits most toilets.

  1. Shut off the water

    Turn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops.

  2. Empty the tank

    Flush and hold the handle, then sponge out what is left.

  3. Disconnect the supply

    Loosen the nut at the bottom of the tank and the nut under the tank that holds the valve.

  4. Remove the old valve

    Lift it out and note the height of the old one.

  5. Install the new valve

    Set it in the hole, tighten the lock nut by hand plus a quarter turn, and connect the supply line.

  6. Adjust the height

    Set the fill line below the top of the overflow tube, then turn the water on and check for leaks.

Fill Valve Types Compared

Most replacement valves fall into two groups.

TypeHow it worksGood for
Float cupA cup rides up the shaft as the tank fillsMost modern toilets, quiet and easy to adjust.
Ball floatA ball on an arm shuts the valveOlder toilets, simple to fix.
DiaphragmA rubber seal inside the valve closes the flowCommon on newer low-flow models.

A universal float cup valve costs little and replaces most older styles, which is why many plumbers carry them on the truck.

Mistakes That Cause a Second Leak

Overtightening the plastic nut cracks the tank, and a cracked tank means a new toilet. Hand tight plus a quarter turn is enough. Set the water level at the mark on the overflow tube, because a level that is too high wastes water down the tube all day.

Check the flapper while you are in there. A worn flapper can look like a fill valve problem, since water leaks into the bowl and the valve refills the tank again. Press a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.

What Highland Water Does to Fill Valves

East Valley Water District's 2026 Consumer Confidence Report lists average hardness at 152 mg/L. Hard water leaves mineral scale on small parts, and a fill valve has a small opening that scale can narrow. If your valve fails every couple of years, scale is a likely cause, and a hardness test of your tap will tell you more.

The City of Highland permit exceptions page lists repairing or replacing a fixture without altering supply or drain lines among minor work. Swapping a fill valve falls in that category, but ask Building and Safety if you are changing anything beyond the toilet itself.

California sets water efficiency standards for new toilets, according to state rules, so a replacement fill valve should be rated for your tank and should not raise the water level above the overflow mark.

Parts and Tools Checklist

Gather these before you shut the water off so the toilet is not out of service longer than needed.

  • A universal fill valveCheck that the height range matches your tank.
  • An adjustable wrench or channel lock pliersFor the nuts at the tank base.
  • A small bucket and a spongeTo catch the last water in the tank.
  • A new supply line if the old one is stiffBraided stainless lines are common.
  • A towelFor the floor under the supply connection.

When to Stop and Call a Plumber

Call a plumber if the shutoff valve will not close, the supply line is corroded, the tank has a crack or water keeps rising after you finish. Those jobs need a new shutoff or a new toilet, and forcing parts can flood a bathroom.

If more than one fixture runs slowly or gurgles, the problem is likely in the drain and not the toilet. See drain cleaning. For repairs beyond the fill valve, our toilet repair page lists what is covered in Highland, Redlands and Mentone.
