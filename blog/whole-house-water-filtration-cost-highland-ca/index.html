Cost of Whole House Water Filtration Systems in Highland, CA

By the Highland Express Plumbing team.Published November 13, 2026.Updated November 13, 2026.

The cost of a whole house water filtration system depends on what you want to remove, the flow rate your home needs and how complex the installation is. A sediment filter costs less than a multi-stage carbon system, and a system that treats hardness adds a softener. Test your water first, because the right system depends on the problem.

We install filters and softeners across Highland. Ask for a written price after a visit rather than a number by phone.

Start With What You Want to Fix

Filters fix different problems. Sediment filters catch sand and rust. Carbon filters reduce chlorine taste and odor. Softeners treat hardness. Reverse osmosis at the kitchen tap treats drinking water.

A whole house system is rarely one device. Many homes use a sediment filter, a carbon stage and a softener in a line. Naming the problem first keeps you from paying for a stage you do not need.

Highland Water Source and Quality

East Valley Water District reports that about 78 percent of its 2026 supply came from Bunker Hill Basin groundwater, with the rest from the State Water Project and Santa Ana River water treated at Plant 134. Its Consumer Confidence Report lists average hardness at 152 mg/L. The report is the right place to start, and a tap test shows what reaches your home.

If your water tastes fine and your concern is scale, a softener may serve you better than a filter. See water softener and filtration for our service.

Filter performance claims should be backed by independent testing to NSF/ANSI standards, so look for the certification mark and read what contaminants it covers.

Common Mistakes That Waste Money

The most common mistake is buying a filter that does not treat the actual problem, such as a carbon filter for scale. Another is skipping a sediment stage and clogging a fine filter in weeks. A third is ignoring flow rate, which can leave you with weak pressure.

A plumber can size the housings to your home and add a bypass so you can service the system without losing water.

Where the System Goes

Most whole house systems mount near the main shutoff, often in a garage or a utility area. The location needs space for cartridge changes, a way to shut off the water on both sides and, for softeners, a drain.

Plan the location before you buy, because a tight closet can limit your options and raise installation labor.

Reading Test Results

A home water test lists hardness, chlorine, iron, pH and other measures. Compare each to the guidance printed on the report. A reading that is flagged high should lead to a treatment plan. A reading in range needs no treatment.

If a result is surprising, retest or ask for a lab test. The district's report covers the water at its source and distribution, and your home piping can add what the district cannot see.

Filters for Wells and Private Sources

Most Highland homes receive district water. Properties on private wells have different needs, and they should test for bacteria, nitrates and metals. A whole house system for a well is more involved and often includes disinfection.

If you are not sure which supply you have, check your bill. The name of the provider is there, and the district's report applies only if you are a customer.

Pressure Loss and Flow Rate

Every filter adds some resistance. A system sized too small can drop pressure at the shower and the washer. Ask for the rated flow in gallons per minute and compare it to your household peak, which is the number of fixtures that may run at once.

Large families and homes with several bathrooms should look at larger housings. A plumber can measure your current pressure and flow, and recommend a size that keeps the house comfortable.

Maintenance and Hidden Costs

Filters need new cartridges on a schedule. A neglected filter can clog and reduce pressure. Ask what the replacement interval is and who supplies cartridges.

Install the system with a bypass so you can service it without shutting the house off. We also install fixtures and valves, see plumbing installation, and this site covers Redlands and Mentone.
