How to Repair a Leaky Bathroom Faucet in Highland, CA

By the Highland Express Plumbing team.Published November 13, 2026.Updated November 13, 2026.

To repair a leaky bathroom faucet, shut off the water at the valves under the sink, remove the handle, pull the cartridge or washer and replace the worn part. Most drips come from a worn washer, seat, cartridge or O-ring, and the fix takes about an hour.

If the shutoff valves will not close or the faucet body is corroded, call a plumber. Timing depends on the plumber and the job, so call and ask. A same-day visit is not promised.

Match the Leak to the Part

Where the water appears tells you what failed.

  • Drip from the spoutA worn washer, seat or cartridge.
  • Water at the base of the handleA worn O-ring or packing nut.
  • Drip only on hot or coldThe cartridge or washer on that side.
  • Water under the sinkA loose or cracked supply line, not the faucet.
  • Weak flow plus a dripScale in the aerator or cartridge.

Fix the Faucet Step by Step

You need a screwdriver, an adjustable wrench, replacement parts and a rag to plug the drain.

  1. Plug the drain

    Place a rag in the drain so small parts cannot fall in.

  2. Shut off the water

    Close the valves under the sink and open the faucet to drain it.

  3. Remove the handle

    Pop off the cap, remove the screw and lift the handle.

  4. Pull the cartridge or washer

    Unscrew the retaining nut and lift out the part.

  5. Replace the worn part

    Take the old part to a hardware store to match it.

  6. Reassemble and test

    Turn the water on slowly and check for drips.

Hard Water and Bathroom Faucets in Highland

East Valley Water District reports average hardness of 152 mg/L in its 2026 Consumer Confidence Report. Hard water leaves white scale that stiffens handles, clogs aerators and wears seals. If the drip returns within a few months, scale on the cartridge is a likely cause, and soaking removable parts in vinegar can clear light buildup.

The City of Highland permit exceptions page lists faucet and fixture repair that does not alter supply or drain lines as minor work. A faucet swap or a part replacement falls in that group. Ask Building and Safety if you are moving lines.

State water efficiency standards also cap how much a new bathroom faucet may flow, according to California rules, so check the flow rating on any replacement.

Tools and Parts to Gather First

A short list saves a second trip to the store.

  • A set of screwdriversPhillips and flat, for handle screws and caps.
  • An adjustable wrenchFor the packing nut and retaining nut.
  • Replacement cartridge or washersBring the old part to match it.
  • Plumber's greaseLightly coats O-rings so they seat and last.
  • White vinegar and a small brushTo clear scale from the aerator and the spout.

Common Mistakes That Bring the Drip Back

The most common mistake is replacing a washer without checking the seat. A scored seat will cut a new washer and the drip returns in days. Another is forcing a stuck handle, which can strip the stem or crack the body.

Lightly grease new O-rings, tighten the nut only until snug and open the supply valves slowly so air in the line does not spit water. If a part is hard to find, the faucet may be a discontinued model, and a new faucet is the cleaner answer.

How Long a Repair Should Last

A good cartridge in water of moderate hardness can last many years. In hard water, expect a shorter life. If you replace a cartridge and the faucet drips again within a year, the faucet body, the seat or scale may be the cause.

At that point a new faucet is the better path, and installation is usually quick. Ask your plumber to also replace the supply lines and the shutoff valves under the sink, since they age at the same pace and a leak there causes real damage.

Water Savings You Can Count

A faucet that drips once per second wastes thousands of gallons in a year, and a drip on a hot line also wastes the energy that heated the water. Fixing it early pays for the part many times over.

While you have the faucet apart, clean the aerator and check that the shutoff valves still turn. A valve that is frozen with scale is worth replacing before it fails during an emergency.

What to do if the faucet will not stop leaking

Limit the water and the damage first.

  • Close the valves under the sinkClockwise until snug.
  • Dry the areaWater under a cabinet can rot the base.
  • Check the supply linesA cracked line leaks even with the faucet off.
  • Do not force a stuck handleIt can crack the body.
  • Call a plumberA damaged seat or body needs more than a washer.

Repair or Replace

A cartridge or washer is a low-cost repair on a faucet in good shape. If the finish is pitted, the body is corroded or parts are no longer made, a new faucet saves repeat visits.
