Installing a Kitchen Sink Garbage Disposal in Highland, CA Homes

By the Highland Express Plumbing team.Published November 13, 2026.Updated November 13, 2026.

Installing a kitchen garbage disposal means mounting a flange in the sink drain, hanging the unit, connecting the drain and the dishwasher hose, and wiring it to a switch. The work is straightforward for a plumber, and a typical replacement takes one visit. A first-time installation may need an outlet, a switch and a drain check.

Timing depends on the plumber and the job, so call and ask. A same-day visit is not promised. Tell us whether you have a dishwasher so we bring the right fittings.

What Installation Involves

The steps are the same for most units.

  1. Remove the old drain

    The sink strainer and trap arm are taken out.

  2. Mount the flange

    The new sink flange is set with putty and a mounting ring.

  3. Hang the disposal

    The unit locks onto the mounting ring.

  4. Connect the drain

    The discharge connects to the trap, and the dishwasher hose attaches to the inlet.

  5. Open the knockout

    A plug in the dishwasher inlet is removed so the dishwasher can drain.

  6. Wire and test

    The power is connected to a switch and the unit is run with water.

The Dishwasher Knockout

Most new disposals have a plastic plug in the dishwasher inlet. If it is not removed, the dishwasher cannot drain, and water sits in the bottom. This is the most common mistake in a do it yourself install.

A plumber removes the plug, connects the hose with a high loop or an air gap and tests the dishwasher on a cycle.

Highland Drains and Permits

The City of Highland permit exceptions page lists repair or replacement of a fixture without altering supply or drain lines as minor work. Swapping an existing disposal falls under that. Adding a disposal where none existed, or changing the drain layout, may be different, so ask Building and Safety.

East Valley Water District reports average hardness of 152 mg/L in 2026, and scale can build on drain parts. If the sink drains slowly after install, the line behind it may be coated, and drain cleaning can help.

Disposal wiring follows the National Electrical Code (NEC) as adopted in California, and the trap and drain follow the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Septic and Other Special Cases

Not every home should have a disposal. Septic systems may limit how much solid waste goes in. Some older drain lines are fragile or slow, and added solids can make clogs worse.

If your sink already drains slowly, have the line checked first. See sewer inspection for a camera look, and ask about maintenance to keep a disposal from causing repeat clogs.

Care After Installation

Run cold water for several seconds before and after you grind. Feed scraps slowly and in small amounts. Avoid fibrous items and grease.

Once a month, grind ice cubes and a little citrus to clear residue. If you notice a smell or a hum, turn the unit off and call us before a small jam becomes a motor failure.

Cost Factors for Installation

The price depends on the unit, the wiring and the condition of the drain. A basic swap of an existing disposal costs less than a first time install that needs a new switch, a new outlet and a new trap.

Older drain parts that are corroded or leaking should be replaced at the same time. It is cheaper to replace a worn trap during the install than to come back for a leak a week later. We quote the unit, the labor and any extra parts before work begins.

Noise, Vibration and Mounting

A well mounted disposal runs with a steady hum. Rattling usually means the mounting ring is loose or a stray utensil is in the chamber. Vibration against the cabinet can be reduced with a rubber sink baffle and by making sure the unit hangs level.

Insulated models run quieter, which matters in an open plan kitchen. Ask about the sound rating of the unit you choose, and keep the manual so you know the reset and jam clearing steps.

Compatibility With Your Sink

Disposals fit standard three and a half inch sink openings. Some stone or composite sinks need a specific flange or a mounting kit. A sink with a thin basin may flex under the weight of a heavy unit.

Tell us the sink material and the number of bowls so the right hardware comes on the first visit.

What to do if the disposal jams or leaks after install

These steps are safe for a homeowner.

  • Turn it off at the switchNever put a hand in the chamber.
  • Unplug it or shut off the breakerBefore you reach under the sink.
  • Use the hex socketTurn it back and forth to free the jam.
  • Press the reset buttonWait a few minutes after a trip.
  • Close the supply valve if water leaksThen call a plumber.

What Not to Put Down the Drain

Grease, coffee grounds in volume, fibrous peels and pasta cause many clogs. Cold water while the unit runs helps flush waste. If the unit jams, turn it off at the switch and use the hex socket at the bottom, never your hand.
