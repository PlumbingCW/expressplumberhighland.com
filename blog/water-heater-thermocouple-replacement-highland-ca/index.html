Water Heater Thermocouple Replacement: A Highland, CA Homeowner Guide

By the Highland Express Plumbing team.Published November 13, 2026.Updated November 13, 2026.

A water heater thermocouple is a small safety sensor that keeps the gas valve open only while the pilot flame is lit. If the pilot lights and then goes out when you release the knob, the thermocouple is the first thing to suspect. Replacement is inexpensive, but the job involves gas, so many homeowners hire a plumber.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility from outside. Do not relight a pilot in a gas smell. For a heater that will not stay lit, call and ask about timing.

Signs of a Failing Thermocouple

These symptoms usually point to the sensor.

  • The pilot lights but dies when you release the knobThe sensor is not holding the valve open.
  • No hot water after a pilot outageThe heater shut off for safety.
  • A pilot flame that looks weak or yellowThe flame may not heat the sensor.
  • A black or bent thermocouple tipThe metal is worn or misaligned.
  • Repeated relightsThe sensor or the flame is inconsistent.

How the Replacement Works

This is the sequence a plumber follows.

  1. Shut off gas and let the heater cool

    The gas control is set to off.

  2. Remove the burner assembly cover

    The pilot assembly is exposed.

  3. Unscrew the old thermocouple

    It connects to the gas valve and sits in the pilot flame.

  4. Install the new one

    The tip is positioned in the flame and the nut is snug.

  5. Relight and test

    We check the flame, the gas connections and the draft.

Garage Heaters and Highland Guidance

The City of Highland's April 2017 Water Heater Installation Bulletin lists an 18 inch elevation for the ignition source of a heater in a garage, along with combustion air, venting, strapping and a drain pan. A pilot flame is an ignition source, so a heater sitting on the floor of a garage may need a platform. The bulletin does not address permits. Ask Building and Safety at 27215 Baseline.

Relighting is not the right fix if the heater has a venting problem. A draft that blows out the pilot can mean a blocked vent. That is a safety issue, and a plumber can check it. See water heater repair.

Replacement parts should match the manufacturer's specifications for your model, according to the heater's manual, and gas connections follow the plumbing and fuel gas rules the state adopts.

Safe Steps If the Pilot Goes Out

Do these before you decide on a repair.

  1. Smell for gas

    If you smell gas, leave the home and call the gas utility from outside.

  2. Check the gas valve and the breaker

    Confirm the gas control is on and the room is ventilated.

  3. Follow the label to relight

    Use the instructions on the heater.

  4. Watch the flame

    A steady blue flame is normal. A weak or yellow flame is not.

  5. Call a plumber if it fails twice

    Repeated outages mean a part or a vent is failing.

Why the Heater Is Not Just a Pilot Problem

A heater that loses its pilot often has another issue behind it. A blocked flue, poor combustion air or sediment in the tank can all cause trouble. A new thermocouple will not fix those causes.

A plumber checks the vent draft, the burner and the tank condition while the part is replaced. That check is what protects you from carbon monoxide, which cannot be seen or smelled.

When a Thermocouple Is Not Enough

If the heater is more than ten years old, rusted at the base or damp around the tank, replacing the thermocouple may only buy a short delay. A leaking tank cannot be repaired. Ask your plumber to judge the whole heater and not only the part.

A new heater also gives you better efficiency and a current safety design, which matters in a garage with an ignition source.

Carbon Monoxide Awareness

Gas heaters produce carbon monoxide as a byproduct. A properly vented heater sends it outside. A blocked vent, a damaged flue or a draft problem can send it into the home. A working carbon monoxide alarm near the sleeping area is a simple protection.

If your alarm sounds, leave the home, call emergency services and do not go back until the source is found. After any heater repair, test the alarm and keep its batteries fresh.

Repair or Replace

A thermocouple is a cheap part, and replacing it is worth it on a heater in good condition. If the tank is leaking, rusting at the base or more than a decade old, replacement is a better use of money.
