A water heater that has failed or started leaking needs replacement fast, so call about it right away. Water heater replacement means draining the old tank, setting a new one to local requirements, reconnecting water, gas or power, and testing everything before we leave.

If your tank is leaking, shut the cold water valve above it and turn off the gas control or the breaker. Then call, because water pooling under a tank damages flooring and drywall the longer it sits.

Replacing a Tank Without Losing Your Weekend

Most replacements take half a day. The plumber confirms the size, fuel and location, removes the old unit, sets the new one and brings the connections up to current requirements, which often means new flexible connectors, a new relief valve and fresh seismic straps.

Timing depends on the job, so ask when you call. Tell the person who answers the tank size on the label, the fuel type and whether it sits in a garage, closet or attic. That lets the plumber arrive with the right unit instead of making a second trip.

How a Replacement Visit Works

These are the steps in order, so there are no surprises at your door.

  1. Confirm the unit

    We read the label for fuel, capacity and venting, and check the space for code requirements.

  2. Agree on the price

    You see the full price before the old tank is touched.

  3. Drain and remove

    The tank is shut off, drained and carried out, and haul-away is arranged.

  4. Set and connect

    The new heater is placed, strapped, vented or wired, and connected with new fittings.

  5. Test and walk through

    We check for leaks, light the burner or heat the elements, and show you the shutoffs.

What Highland's Building Division Says About Water Heaters

The City of Highland publishes a water heater installation bulletin. It calls for seismic strapping in the upper and lower thirds of the tank, with at least 4 inches between the lower strap and the controls. In attics, on subfloors or in floor and ceiling assemblies it requires a corrosion-resistant drain pan at least 1.5 inches deep, piped to the exterior or another approved spot through a drain of at least 3/4 inch.

In a garage, burners must sit at least 18 inches above the floor unless the heater is rated for flammable vapor ignition resistance. The relief valve drain has to end between 6 and 24 inches above grade outside the building. The bulletin does not address permits, so ask the Highland Building and Safety Division whether your replacement needs one. Owners in newer planned communities can read local notes on the East Highlands Ranch page.

A replacement heater is installed according to the manufacturer's instructions and the plumbing code, and the City of Highland's April 2017 bulletin lists the local items inspectors look at.

Hard Water and Choosing the Right Unit

East Valley Water District's 2026 report lists average hardness of 152 mg/L for Highland water. Scale from hard water shortens the life of tanks and clogs tankless heat exchangers, so the unit you choose should match that reality.

A tank with a larger anode rod and an annual flush plan can run well in hard water. A tankless unit works best with a descaling schedule, or a water softener ahead of it. We talk through the trade-off before you commit.

What Affects the Price of a Replacement

The price depends on a few things you can check before help arrives.

Tank size and fuelA 50 gallon gas unit costs more than a 40 gallon electric one.
Efficiency levelHigher efficiency models cost more now and less to run.
Venting and gas workA new vent run or gas line change adds materials and labor.
Code upgradesStraps, a pan, a relief valve drain or an expansion tank may be needed.
Access and haul-awayAn attic or tight closet takes longer, so ask whether haul-away of the old unit is included in the quote.

Your homeowners policy may cover resulting water damage but rarely covers the heater itself. Take photos of the leak before cleanup.
